# Fact Bank additions — DRAFT for review (not used by the site)

Every entry is based only on search-result text seen during this research. Nothing was opened directly (WebFetch unavailable), so all URLs are as returned by search.

## A. 1937, hour by hour
- **N1.** On July 2, 1937, Amelia Earhart and navigator Fred Noonan took off from Lae, New Guinea, bound for tiny Howland Island, a flight of roughly 2,500 miles. — Sources: [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/node/176348), [Smithsonian NASM story](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten). Confidence: high. Note: distance varies by source (2,556 statute miles per Smithsonian summary; another source gives 2,243 nautical miles / about 2,581 statute miles). Say "about 2,500 miles".
- **N2.** The takeoff was at 10:00 a.m. Lae local time, which is midnight (0000) Greenwich time (GMT/UTC) on July 2. — Sources: [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/node/176348), [TIGHAR Log Jam](https://tighar.org/Publications/TTracks/12_2/logjam.html). Confidence: high (local time), medium (GMT, TIGHAR-attributed). Note: TIGHAR also records a telegram saying noon Lae time and a Radio Suva report of 9:30 a.m.; the 10:00 / 0000 GMT figure is the one both Chater and Collopy are said to agree on. One blog claimed midnight local; treat as wrong.
- **N3.** The Electra carried about 1,100 gallons of fuel, a very heavy load for an expected flight of around 18 hours. — Sources: [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/node/176348) (18-hour expectation), [Thisdayinaviation / other secondary, via search](https://www.thisdayinaviation.com/2-july-1937/). Confidence: medium. Note: the 1,100-gallon figure appeared in secondary sources only; a West Point paper estimates about 1,092 standard gallons after heat effects. Say "about 1,100 gallons". Do not use "50 percent overloaded".
- **N4.** The US Coast Guard cutter Itasca waited just off Howland Island to help with radio contact, weather reports and direction-finding. — Sources: [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/node/176348), [Thisdayinaviation](https://www.thisdayinaviation.com/2-july-1937/). Confidence: high. Note: radio contact was poor; no two-way voice or Morse contact was ever made (Smithsonian).
- **N5.** Itasca kept its clocks on ship time, 11.5 hours ahead of Greenwich, while Earhart worked in Greenwich time. That is why the logs and the pilots' times don't line up easily. — Sources: [TIGHAR Itasca radio logs](https://tighar.org/Projects/Earhart/Archives/Research/Bulletins/37_ItascaLogs/background.html). Confidence: medium. Note: TIGHAR only; a separate TIGHAR piece puts Howland clock time at 11:30 for the 10:00 Lae departure, consistent with this. The name of the zone was not confirmed.
- **N6.** At 7:42 a.m. (Itasca time), Itasca logged Earhart saying, in effect, "We must be on you but cannot see you. Fuel is running low. Been unable to reach you by radio. We are flying at 1,000 feet." — Sources: [National Archives, Earhart radio log](https://www.archives.gov/college-park/highlights/earhart-log), [DocsTeach](https://docsteach.org/document/earhart-radio-log/). Confidence: medium-high. Note: the quoted text came from a search-result summary; confirm exact wording against the National Archives log scan. 7:42 a.m. Itasca time is about 20:12 GMT on July 1 by the 11.5-hour offset (calculated, not sourced; verify before using).
- **N7.** Earhart was heard giving a line of position, "We are on the line 157 337 ... will repeat message ...", and this is usually treated as the last confirmed message. — Sources: [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/college-park/highlights/earhart-log), [TIGHAR Transmission timeline](https://tighar.org/wiki/Transmission_timeline). Confidence: medium. Note: logged at 8:43 a.m. Itasca time. TIGHAR argues the log was amended and the call may not have been exactly at 8:43. Wording from TIGHAR wiki is a secondary compilation.
- **N8.** Itasca's logs show nothing more was heard from the plane after that call; the expected fuel-out time of about 8:10 had already passed. — Sources: [TIGHAR Itasca logs](https://tighar.org/Projects/Earhart/Archives/Research/Bulletins/37_ItascaLogs/background.html). Confidence: medium. Note: TIGHAR's phrasing; its 8:10 time is its own reading, so attribute it. UNVERIFIED as a Navy/USCG statement.
- **N9.** Within about two hours of losing contact, Itasca began searching north and northwest of Howland and kept at it for about a week. — Sources: [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html). Confidence: medium. Note: single secondary source; USCG history page not found in search.
- **N10.** The US Navy joined the search with the battleship Colorado, the carrier Lexington, the seaplane tender Swan and three destroyers (Cushing, Lamson, Drayton). — Sources: [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html). Confidence: medium. Note: secondary source; verify against NHHC.
- **N11.** A Navy report released by the National Archives says the ships swept nearly 250,000 square miles of ocean, and patrol seaplanes covered about 25,000 square miles a day. — Sources: [Fox 5 DC (syndicated)](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/government-releases-newly-declassified-amelia-earhart-disappearance-records-from-national-archives), [National Archives Earhart page](https://archives.gov/news/topics/earhart). Confidence: medium. Note: figures came from news coverage of the release (4,624 pages, Nov 2025), not directly from the Archives page. Prefer primary confirmation.
- **N12.** The official US search was called off on July 18, 1937, with no trace of the plane or crew. The press estimated the cost at $4 million and called it the largest search of its kind up to then. — Sources: [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html), [National Archives Earhart page](https://archives.gov/news/topics/earhart). Confidence: high (July 18), medium ($4M is a press estimate). Note: UNVERIFIED: "66 aircraft" and "4,000 men" figures were NOT found; do not use.

## B. Earhart's Purdue years
- **N13.** Purdue President Edward C. Elliott heard Earhart speak in New York about careers for women and invited her to visit Purdue. Her appointment as a visiting faculty member was arranged in 1935. — Sources: [Purdue Libraries archives](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/agents/people/1133?page=1), [Purdue press memorandum 1935](https://digital.library.in.gov/Record/PU_epurdue-155). Confidence: medium-high. Note: announcement dated June 3, 1935 per TIGHAR; TIGHAR says she did not actually start until November 7, 1935. One Purdue record says association began 1936, which conflicts. Say "joined in 1935".
- **N14.** From autumn 1935 until her disappearance she served as Consultant in the Department for the Study of Careers for Women and Technical Advisor in the Department of Aeronautics. — Sources: [Purdue Libraries archives](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/agents/people/1133?page=1). Confidence: high.
- **N15.** At Purdue she lived in the new women's residence hall for part of each semester, counseled students on careers, advised aeronautical engineering, and could use Purdue's new airport. — Sources: [Purdue Libraries archives](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/agents/people/1133?page=1), [Earhart Residence Hall records](https://asc03p.lib.purdue.edu/repositories/2/resources/439). Confidence: medium. Note: "few weeks of the semester" came from a search summary.
- **N16.** In April 1936 a fund of $50,000, the Amelia Earhart Fund for Aeronautical Research, was announced, run through the Purdue Research Foundation, to buy her Electra. — Sources: [TIGHAR, Electra book ch. 2](https://TIGHAR.org/Publications/Books/ElectraBook/chaptertwo.pdf), [Purdue Research Foundation stories](https://stories.prf.org/purdue-expedition-amelia-earhart/). Confidence: medium. Note: $50,000 is a TIGHAR-quoted fund total. Plane cost is inconsistent: Purdue materials say $80,000; another source says just over $40,000 came from the fund. UNVERIFIED which is right; avoid quoting a plane price.
- **N17.** The Electra was nicknamed the "Flying Laboratory" and Earhart and George Putnam said they meant to return it to Purdue after the flight. — Sources: [Purdue Research Foundation stories](https://stories.prf.org/purdue-expedition-amelia-earhart/), [TIGHAR Electra book](https://TIGHAR.org/Publications/Books/ElectraBook/chaptertwo.pdf). Confidence: medium (overlaps F7/F8, may be redundant).
- **N18.** Purdue Libraries holds the George Palmer Putnam Collection of Amelia Earhart Papers (MSP 9), mostly from 1928-1937, with scrapbooks and artifacts, plus a digitized online version. — Sources: [Purdue collection description](https://collections.lib.purdue.edu/aearhart/collectiondescription.php), [Purdue finding aid PDF](https://archives.lib.purdue.edu/fa/msp9_earhart_a.pdf). Confidence: high. Note: original is closed to researchers. Extent differs (16.5 vs 31.1 cubic feet); a 2002 gift from Sally Putnam Chapman added 492 items; Putnam gift in 1940. Avoid volume figures.

## C. Nikumaroro, the island
- **N19.** Nikumaroro is a small coral atoll, about 7.5 km by 2.5 km per NASA, with a large lagoon in the middle. — Sources: [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll). Confidence: medium. Note: other sources give roughly 6 km by 2 km or 4.5 by 1.5 miles; use "a few miles long".
- **N20.** The lagoon has only two narrow openings to the sea, so it is murky and acts as a nursery for sharks. — Sources: [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/places/nikumaroro). Confidence: medium. Note: single source seen (via summary).
- **N21.** The atoll was once called Gardner Island, a name given by American sailors. — Sources: [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll). Confidence: high.
- **N22.** UNVERIFIED: location of the Taraia Peninsula on the atoll. Search found only that the Taraia Object lies in the lagoon. — Sources: [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/amelia-earhart-lost-plane-new-expedition-location-nikumaroro-pacific). Confidence: not confirmed. Note: do not state which side of the atoll without a map source.
- **N23.** A British colony of Gilbertese settlers began on the island in 1938 as part of the Phoenix Islands Settlement Scheme and ended in 1963 after severe drought, when the settlers were resettled in the Solomon Islands. — Sources: [Phoenix Islands Settlement Scheme (Wikipedia mirror)](https://wikipedia2006.classicistranieri.com/en/p/h/o/Phoenix_Islands_Settlement_Scheme_e8c9.html), [TIGHAR Research Bulletin 25](https://tighar.org/Projects/Earhart/Archives/Research/Bulletins/25_GallagherNiku/25_GallagherNiku.html). Confidence: medium. Note: sources vary on start (scheme approved 1937; settlers there Dec 1938). Better source wanted (UK/Kiribati official or Britannica).
- **N24.** The British steamer SS Norwich City ran onto the reef in 1929 (TIGHAR dates it the night of November 29); eleven crew died and 24 survivors were rescued about four days later. — Sources: [TIGHAR FAQ](https://tighar.org/Projects/Earhart/Archives/Forum/FAQs/shipwreck.htm), [Wikipedia: SS Norwich City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Norwich_City). Confidence: medium-high. Note: crew totals differ between sources. Not using TIGHAR hypothesis about the Electra landing there.
- **N25.** The island is part of the Phoenix Islands Protected Area (PIPA), declared by Kiribati in 2006. — Sources: [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll), [Big Ocean Managers](https://bigoceanmanagers.org/pipa). Confidence: medium. Note: formal regulations 2008.
- **N26.** PIPA became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010 (site 1325), covering 408,250 square kilometres, then the largest marine World Heritage Site. — Sources: [IUCN World Heritage Outlook](https://worldheritageoutlook.iucn.org/node/1160), [Guinness World Records](https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/771546-largest-world-heritage-site-by-area). Confidence: medium-high. Note: official UNESCO page (whc.unesco.org/en/list/1325) not seen. IUCN's Oct 2025 outlook flags "significant concern" after much of the area was degazetted for commercial fishing; check current status before saying "protected".

## Gaps and cautions
- Taraia Peninsula's position on the atoll: unconfirmed.
- No history.uscg.mil or history.navy.mil narrative pages came up; USCG and Navy details lean on Pacific Wrecks and syndicated news. Seek primary pages.
- "66 aircraft / 4,000 men" and any exact search cost: not found.
- Time zones: Lae 10:00 = 0000 GMT; Itasca ship time = GMT+11.5 (TIGHAR). Howland-based logs are contested; the Howland DF log reportedly runs a day off. Avoid conversions we did not see sourced.
- Last transmission time (0843) is disputed by TIGHAR; attribute the dispute to TIGHAR.
- Image licenses must be checked on the actual Commons/NASA pages; I never opened them.
- The Norwich City / Electra reef link is TIGHAR's hypothesis; keep it out of facts.
