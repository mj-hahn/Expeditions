Status key:

- **VERIFIED**: the claim appears on the opened source page, quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **CHANGED**: the source supports a corrected or narrower version (shown).
- **BLOCKED**: the source couldn't be opened from here (domain not allowed, or rate-limited), so it's still unverified.

## A. 1937, hour by hour

- **N1. VERIFIED.** On July 2, 1937, Earhart and Noonan took off from Lae, New Guinea, for Howland Island, an easterly flight of about 2,556 miles (4,113 km). — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten)
- **N2. VERIFIED.** Takeoff was at 10:00 a.m. Lae time, which was midnight (00:00) Greenwich time. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten) (10:00 local); [TIGHAR, "Log Jam"](https://tighar.org/Publications/TTracks/12_2/logjam.html) (00:00 Greenwich)
- **N3. CHANGED.** TIGHAR's reconstruction puts about 1,100 U.S. gallons of fuel aboard at takeoff; she expected to fly at least 18 hours and arrive around sunrise. — [TIGHAR, "Log Jam"](https://tighar.org/Publications/TTracks/12_2/logjam.html) (fuel, attribute to TIGHAR); [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten) (18 hours, sunrise)
- **N4. VERIFIED.** The Coast Guard cutter Itasca waited at Howland with fuel. Howland is only about 2 miles long and 1 mile wide. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten)
- **N4b. VERIFIED (new).** Earhart and the Itasca never made two-way contact. Confused radio plans, and the fact that neither she nor Noonan could use Morse code, meant she may never have heard the ship's calls. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten)
- **N5. DROP for now.** Itasca's ship time (GMT+11.5). The source is a TIGHAR page that's rate-limited. Not needed if we give times "as logged by the Itasca".
- **N6. VERIFIED (log wording).** At 7:42 a.m. (Itasca's log time) the radio log reads, as typed: "WE MUST ON ON YOU BUT CANNOT SEE U BUT GAS IS RUNNING LOW BEEN UNABLE TO REACH YOU BY RADIO WE ARE FLYING AT A 1000 FEET." — [TIGHAR, "Log Jam"](https://tighar.org/Publications/TTracks/12_2/logjam.html), transcribing the Itasca Primary Radio Log. *Show it as typed, then the familiar paraphrase "We must be on you but cannot see you."*
- **N6b. VERIFIED (new).** Her strongest signal came at 7:58 a.m. She was circling, searching and running low on fuel. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten)
- **N7. VERIFIED.** At 8:43 a.m. the log reads "WE ARE ON THE LINE 157 337 WE WL REPT." That was the last transmission the Itasca received; no one heard from her again. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten) (last at 0843); [TIGHAR, "Log Jam"](https://tighar.org/Publications/TTracks/12_2/logjam.html) (wording). *TIGHAR notes the log time was struck over and altered; mention only if attributed.*
- **N8. DROP.** "Fuel-out about 8:10" is TIGHAR's own estimate, and the source is rate-limited.
- **N9. VERIFIED.** About two hours after losing contact, the Itasca began searching north and northwest of Howland, and kept at it for the next week. — [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html)
- **N10. VERIFIED.** The U.S. Navy joined with the battleship Colorado, the seaplane tender Swan, the carrier Lexington and three destroyers (Cushing, Lamson and Drayton). — [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html)
- **N11. BLOCKED.** "~250,000 square miles searched." The sources (www.archives.gov, fox5dc.com) aren't reachable. Leave out unless `www.archives.gov` is allowed.
- **N12. VERIFIED.** The U.S. government searched from July 2 to July 18, its largest ever search for a lost aircraft, and found no trace of the plane or crew. — [Smithsonian NASM](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/amelia-earhart-missing-80-years-not-forgotten). *Optional: the press estimated the cost at $4 million; the real cost was never officially calculated.* — [Pacific Wrecks](https://pacificwrecks.com/aircraft/electra/1055.html)

## C. Nikumaroro, the island

- **N19. VERIFIED.** Nikumaroro stretches about 7.5 km by 2.5 km (4.7 by 1.6 miles), surrounded by deep ocean. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N19b. VERIFIED (new).** It's essentially a sandbar on a coral reef atop a sinking deep-sea volcano. Thick scrub, Pisonia trees and coconut palms hold the sand in place around a central lagoon. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N19c. VERIFIED (new).** It lies nearly 1,000 miles from Fiji, roughly halfway between Australia and Hawaii. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N20. BLOCKED.** Lagoon openings and sharks. The source (Atlas Obscura) isn't allowed.
- **N21. VERIFIED.** It was once named Gardner Island by American sailors. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N21b. VERIFIED (new).** Americans and the British tried several times to settle the island, growing coconuts and even considering it for weapons testing. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N22. DROP.** Taraia Peninsula's location: no source found.
- **N23. CHANGED.** In the late 1930s the island was settled under Britain's Phoenix Islands Settlement Scheme; progress reports date from December 1938. — [TIGHAR Research Bulletin 25](https://tighar.org/Projects/Earhart/Archives/Research/Bulletins/25_GallagherNiku/25_GallagherNiku.html). *The 1963 end is already in F15. The "drought" and "Solomon Islands" details weren't on the opened page.*
- **N24. BLOCKED.** SS Norwich City wreck (1929). The TIGHAR FAQ is rate-limited and Wikipedia isn't allowed.
- **N25. VERIFIED.** Kiribati declared the island part of the Phoenix Islands Protected Area in 2006. — [NASA Earth Observatory](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/91889/nikumaroro-atoll)
- **N26. BLOCKED.** UNESCO World Heritage (2010). whc.unesco.org and iucn.org aren't allowed. The draft also flagged a 2025 change in protection status.
